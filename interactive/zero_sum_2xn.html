Two-player zero-sum games with two rows

Player 1 chooses one of two rows and Player 2 one of n columns; the entries of U1 below are Player 1's payoffs, and Player 2 receives their negative. Edit the entries, or add and remove columns, and the plot and the solution below update. Entries may be integers, decimals, or fractions such as 3/4; equilibrium solutions and snapped readouts are exact. Plot coordinates and moving-slider readouts are approximate.

Graphical solution

Write Player 1's strategy as x = (t, 1 − t). Against column j, Player 1 earns ℓj(t), a linear function of t. Player 2 has a pure best response to any x, so x guarantees the lower envelope minj ℓj(t), drawn in black. The maxmin strategies of Player 1 are its highest points. Dashed lines mark columns strictly dominated for Player 2 by another pure column: that other column gives Player 1 a smaller payoff in both rows.

Focus a matrix entry or hover a column to follow its line and LP constraint.

Solution

Explore Player 2’s strategy

An optimal strategy holds both row payoffs at or below the value. A best response to one particular row mixture need not do that.

y = (1 − λ) A + λ B. Every point on this segment is optimal. With more than two optimal endpoints, these segments need not cover every optimal strategy.

Linear programming certificate

At a pair of optimal solutions, a positive weight sits only on a constraint that holds with equality (complementary slackness). The converse can fail: a constraint can hold with equality while its weight is 0.
