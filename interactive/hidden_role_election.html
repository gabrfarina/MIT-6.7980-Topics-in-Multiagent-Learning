Hidden-role election

Take a randomly assigned seat in the three-player election. You will receive a private role, make one public claim, and vote. The other two players follow the strategies from the notes. Electing any Good player gives Good the round; electing Bad or splitting the vote gives it to Bad.

What to look for

With indistinguishable Good cards, Bad can say “Good” and copy the Good voting rule. The public record then looks the same no matter who is Bad, so electing an arbitrary player wins about two thirds of the time — the value in the notes.

A Good player need not elect themself: any Good winner is enough. Voting for yourself is safe individually, but if both Good players do that, the three votes can split and Bad wins. The team therefore needs a shared coordination rule. Bad, by contrast, wants to be elected and votes for themself.

Mark the Good cards Good and Good' and keep the same payoffs. If the Good seats announce honestly and elect the unique remaining label, Bad cannot match both labels. Whenever you are dealt Bad in this mode, try all three kinds of claim; the two Good players can still defeat you.
